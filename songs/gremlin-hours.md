# GREMLIN HOURS

*Chicago underground rage-trap / dark plugg, supernatural horror, 3 AM*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tempo** | 152 BPM (switches to a 76 half-time feel in the darker sections) |
| **Key** | F♯ minor, with Phrygian flat-2 (G natural) in the piano |
| **Length** | about 3:10 |
| **Vocals** | Male. Detached but animated, slightly sinister. Whispered doubles, stacked ghost harmonies, and bit-crushed distortion on some punchlines |

---

## Production map

| Time | Section | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Intro** | Detuned music-box piano, low and far away, with tape wow. Reversed choir pad. A wet footstep sample panned slowly L→R. Whispered counting sits under the mix. No drums. |
| 0:14 | **Hook 1** | An 808 slides in from below (glide down a fifth). Hats come in sparse at first, then roll in triplets. A clap with a short, dark plate. Ghost-vocal stack at three octaves, one layer reversed. |
| 0:36 | **Verse 1** | Bouncy plugg pocket: bell lead, a skippy 808 pattern, and a hi-hat that drops out every 4th bar so the vocal sits alone. A creaking-door sample fills the gaps. |
| 1:12 | **Hook 2** | Same as Hook 1, plus a distorted 808 layer and a chopped vocal "find me" stuttering at 1/32 notes. |
| 1:34 | **Bridge / Switch** | Everything cuts out except the piano and one breath. Then the half-time drop: blown-out 808, a slow kick, metallic foley percussion (chains, a radiator tick), and a sub that detunes downward like it's sinking. |
| 1:58 | **Verse 2** | Back to 152 with an aggressive rage synth (a supersaw through a bitcrusher, sidechained hard). The rapid-fire part rides 1/16 hats, then the beat stops dead for the pause bars. |
| 2:34 | **Hook 3** | Full stack. Voices panned all the way around the listener. An extra octave-down whisper doubles every line. |
| 2:56 | **Outro** | Drums drop out. The piano is pitched down a half step and keeps sliding. Whispered countdown. A door shuts, then room tone, then nothing. |

**Sound design notes:** Avoid stock trap rolls. Vary the hat pattern every 2 bars and put one bar of silence where the listener expects a roll. Let the 808 glides land a beat late now and then so the low end feels alive. Sprinkle very quiet laughter, a distant train (the L), wind through a gangway, and a TV left on in another room. Keep the vocals raw: light tuning, no heavy polish, and audible breaths.
